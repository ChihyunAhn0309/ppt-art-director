# Live template search and adaptation

Use this route for a new deck or substantial redesign, including a short example on a previously discussed subject. Work with live provider pages and selected files for this brief; no RAG system, vector database, server, bulk crawler or permanent catalogue is required. Preserve an existing design when the user explicitly selects it or requests a targeted edit to an identified deck with that design retained. An earlier example cited for its topic, data or useful diagram does not by itself select its entire design.

## Search for the content problem

Translate the brief into the subject, audience, hardest slide structure, content density, language and tone. Search several relevant providers or creators when accessible; compare meaningfully different composition families, not only color variants of one deck. Use [reference-library.md](reference-library.md) as a starting point and expand beyond it when results are weak.

Combine subject and structural queries. For a compression talk, search both a technical research presentation and a method flow or latency–accuracy comparison. A template need not mention the exact research topic if its content geometry is better suited. English searches can widen discovery for Korean decks; they do not set the output language. Search snippets, popularity and a “premium” or “AI-generated” label are not quality evidence.

Keep a small working shortlist. Inspect a relevant body/evidence slide, the densest candidate layout and the overall type/spacing system, using provider previews or a rendered original. Include opening/closing treatment where it affects the direction. Record which pages were actually seen and at what resolution. Expand the search when candidates cannot carry the required evidence; stop when further browsing would not change a defensible choice. There is no template quota or universal aesthetic score.

## Choose a usable source

Apply two selection gates: **fit for this subject and its evidence**, and **design quality after the real content is inserted**. Free access, a matching topic keyword and editable objects are eligibility conditions, not reasons to prefer a weak design. Judge hierarchy, typography, spacing, proportions, useful grouping and rhythm together. A restrained template should still feel deliberately composed; oversized empty regions, disconnected labels or generic boxes do not become polished merely because they are uncluttered. Conversely, decorative complexity is not evidence of quality.

Compare the strongest candidates on representative body slides, not covers alone. Explain concretely why the selected design suits this subject and why its composition is stronger for the actual content than the alternatives. Do not default to a generic academic look solely because the subject is research, or to the easiest file to manipulate. If the inspected options only satisfy access/editability or lose their visual quality after replacement, expand the search or reconsider the layout before committing. Keep this a brief design judgment, not a fabricated numerical score or extra approval round.

Evaluate candidates in context:

| Decision | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Content fit | Required table rows, method relationships, equations and qualifiers have readable space. A beautiful cover cannot compensate for unsuitable body layouts. |
| Composition | Clear focal object, supporting hierarchy, useful grouping, deliberate proportions and consistent alignment; no irrelevant decoration needed to make the design work. |
| Actual access and terms | The chosen download/export is available without an unapproved purchase; its current terms cover the intended adaptation and delivery, including attribution and embedded assets. |
| Native editing | Inspect actual text, shapes, tables/charts, masters and layouts. A `.pptx` extension or “editable” label does not prove editable content. A slide-sized image with no native text is a visual reference, not an editable template. |
| Language and density | Real English/Korean copy fits with available fonts and meaningful phrase breaks. Preserve interpretation and evidence rather than forcing a literal translation into fixed boxes. |
| Deck consistency | Choose a primary design family and compatible supporting patterns; reconcile type, palette, margins and chart grammar before combining sources. |

Use the creator/provider's original page and download link. Check item-level and provider terms; a repository license may exclude bundled assets. Keep required attribution in an allowed location. If use terms are unclear or access needs an unavailable account, select another usable source. Do not buy, sign up, bypass access limits or build an archive just to finish a deck. Metadata, screenshots and extracted assets also need an appropriate basis for reuse; converting them to embeddings or geometry does not create permission.

Assess the intended use precisely: making the user's own presentation, delivering a completed deck, and publishing a reusable template for download are different activities. A ban on template redistribution or database inclusion does not by itself establish that permitted personal/work presentation use is forbidden; likewise, permission to present does not automatically permit a public editable download. Record the applicable permission and remaining delivery limits. Decorative sample pictures or flourishes are not an automatic reason to reject a useful native layout: remove or replace them when the terms allow, then judge the resulting content hierarchy.

## Retrieve, inspect and adapt

When a suitable, permitted original is available, retrieve the selected PPTX into the task's working directory and inspect it before committing the plan to that route. Downloading a candidate for inspection does not mean the final deck has been produced. Preserve the original and work on a copy. Do not download whole provider collections or vendor third-party templates into this skill repository.

Inventory slide size, master/layout relationships, fonts, native objects, sample content, notes, links and timing. Use [production.md](production.md) and the host's supported template-editing route. Fit the user's content to the appropriate layouts while preserving useful template structure. Replace sample values, charts, fictional claims and placeholder identities; retain only audience-relevant material and required credits. Keep supplied evidence independent of the template's example content.

Keep the source's useful visual identity: layout proportions, type hierarchy, spacing, palette and treatment of evidence. A good free template may be used largely as designed, with content replacement, Korean font support and necessary figure changes. There is no requirement to make it look newly invented. Do not strip the source down and recreate the previous deck's split cover, palette and process field because those components are already coded. If removing irrelevant decoration destroys the design's usefulness, choose a better-fitting source rather than claiming that the old layout is the chosen template.

If the selected file is flattened, cannot preserve required features or is not licensed for the intended use, try another plausible source within the bounded search. When no suitable editable original is available, independently compose native slides from general observed design principles and identify that route accurately, recording the actual access, rights, editing or content-fit gap. Convenience, a familiar generator, the same subject and a short deadline are not evidence of that gap. Do not trace protected artwork, lift assets or reproduce a restricted template through reconstruction. Do not claim an original was adapted if it was only downloaded/inspected and every slide was then rebuilt in an unrelated style. Faithful user-required template work needs a compatible source/engine; disclose the specific gap instead of silently substituting.

## Carry the choice through the plan and final file

Record the chosen source URL, access date, inspected pages, why it fits the hardest slide, reuse terms, native-editing evidence and any rejected traits in the [reference ledger](../assets/design-reference-template.md). Map the useful layouts or principles to Sxx IDs. A brief candidate comparison belongs in the plan; do not turn it into a mandatory template-picker approval round. Keep the normal plan-first feedback checkpoint. In explicit one-shot mode, select and proceed yourself.

Prototype the hardest slide with actual copy in the selected source layout. Verify its content, fit and design before expanding. Retain a source/body-before and final/body-after comparison in the task's private QA files. Check what survived from the chosen design and whether the content is readable; a color swap, source URL or passing package check alone does not establish template use. Then inspect every slide and each language edition. A template is a starting system, not a guarantee of a polished output after content replacement. Follow [quality.md](quality.md); preserve the user's private-brief exclusions and distinguish saved native animation settings from observed playback.

In the handoff, name and link the template actually used, or state the precise reference-only/fallback route. Keep this short and outside audience slide copy, apart from required source credits. This lets the user distinguish a real external-template adaptation from another version of a previous deck.

The delivered deck should work independently of the search session. Keep sources and rights evidence with the plan, retain required credits, and report source-access or font limitations that materially affect the result. This workflow adds no required hosting or paid template subscription; the host agent and its tools may have their own costs and limits.
